Free guitar ear training

Hear a note. Name it.

By Vaibhav Verma · Updated 14 September 2026

Earstep is a beginner method for learning notes by listening, plus a free trainer in the browser. You do not need an account. You do not start with all twelve notes.

Open the trainer

What this site is

There are two parts. The pages you can read — this home page and the guides — explain how to train your ear. The trainer is the drill: it plays a guitar or piano note, you name it, and you can find it on a fretboard. Ads, if they appear, sit on these written pages. They do not sit on the listening drill.

I made this because most ear trainers I used felt like a test I had already failed. They played any pitch from the chromatic set and asked for a letter. Accuracy stayed near chance. The missing idea was not “try harder.” It was “use a smaller set.”

The first skill is two sounds

C is a little lower. D is one step higher. That pair is enough for a first week. When you can name them without watching a diagram, add E. Then F. The seven natural letters — C D E F G A B — come after those pairs feel boring in a good way.

A letter is the pitch class. The octave is the height. C4 and C5 are both C. Beginners mix those jobs and answer “high” when the question was “which name?” The trainer splits them: name the letter first, then a separate Low / Middle / High drill.

What a first session looks like

  1. Open the trainer. Choose C and D if you are new.
  2. Play a note. Name it. Replay as much as you want.
  3. When you are ready, find that letter on the guitar. High e is at the top of the board, low E at the bottom, like a tab.
  4. Stop at ten or fifteen minutes. Tired guessing teaches guessing.

Progress stays in this browser. Clearing site data clears it. There is no login and no cloud scoreboard.

Guitar, piano, and sargam

The names are the same on guitar and piano. The geography is not. Piano is one key per pitch. Guitar repeats pitches on more than one string. After you can name a note, the useful extra question is “where can I play it?” The trainer’s fretboard plays the tapped pitch so the picture and the sound stay tied together.

Settings can show Western letters, solfège, or sargam. If Sa is C, then C is Sa and D is Re. The listening work does not change when the labels change.

Who it is for

People who hold a guitar or sit at a piano and want to recognize what they hear before they memorize another chart. It is not a full theory course, not a tab archive, and not a social app. If you already have solid relative pitch, the Journey map will feel easy — skip ahead, or use custom practice.
