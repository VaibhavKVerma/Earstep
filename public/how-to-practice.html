How to practice ear training

By Vaibhav Verma · Updated 14 September 2026

Ear training improves when the set is small and the minutes are honest. This is a plan you can repeat, not a challenge to finish the internet.

Open today’s practice

Keep the session shorter than your patience

Fifteen focused minutes is plenty. If you are guessing, you are done — not because you failed, but because tired ears learn noise. Earstep does not punish a replay. Use that. The first listen is the real test. Extra listens are how the sound settles.

Practice at the same time of day if you can. Hearing is a habit. A streak on the home screen is only a reminder. The work is the listen.

The daily stack

  1. Notes. Your current pair or trio only. Name the letter. Ignore how high it is until this step is easy.
  2. Height. Two or three Low / Middle / High questions so octave does not stay a mystery.
  3. Guitar. Hear one note, tap a fret. Several places can be right unless you asked for the exact pitch.
  4. A phrase. On some days, a melody or a public-domain song line. Not every day. Phrases are dessert after the dictionary.

Daily training on the home screen is this mix already. Customize if you want fewer questions or a different instrument. Acoustic guitar is a good default if you hold a guitar. Piano is a good default if you sit at keys.

When to add a note

Add a letter when the current set is boring in a good way — you are not surprised, you are confirming. If C and D still feel like a guess, do not add E to “cover more material.” Coverage is not the goal. Recognition is.

The Journey map waits for steady accuracy before it unlocks the next level. You can still practice anything. The lock is a suggestion that the foundation is not done.

What to do with a weak note

If F is the one you miss, do not only run a full seven-note quiz. Practice F against its neighbors: E and G. Isolation plus neighbors teaches the border. Earstep’s Journey can send you at a weak note on purpose. Take that offer.

Environment

Use headphones if the room is noisy. Turn off a second video. Set volume so the note is clear, not loud. If you sing, hum after you hear, then find the fret. The voice is a bridge. The guitar is the landing.

A sample week

Monday: C vs D, then find them on guitar. Tuesday: same, plus three octave questions. Wednesday: add E if Tuesday was easy. Thursday: Hear → Sing → Find once. Friday: a short melody. Saturday: rest or a light review. Sunday: one song phrase, then stop.
